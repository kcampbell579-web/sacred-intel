_Disclosure: I run a company that sells home environmental testing kits, including for lead. I’m not telling you to buy one. Disclosing anyway._

SQT recalled about 1,835 Lontcs children’s busy boards on September 10. They were sold on Amazon from May 2024 through July 2026 for roughly $31. The problem is the zipper. It contains lead above the federal lead paint ban limit. No injuries reported. CPSC says stop using it immediately and get a full refund; the company wants you to take the board apart, write “RECALLED” on it, and email them a photo before you throw it out.

A busy board, if you don’t have a toddler: a panel of latches, buckles, switches, and zippers that a two-year-old can fiddle with. The entire product is designed to be handled, mouthed, and pulled on by the one age group most vulnerable to lead. And the lead is in the part with the most hand contact.


## Why the zipper

Lead shows up in metal hardware more than people expect. It’s used in some brass and zinc alloys because it makes the metal easier to machine, and it’s cheap. On a zipper pull that a toddler chews on, that’s a direct oral exposure route. This isn’t paint on a surface; it’s the substrate. It doesn’t wear off. It’s there for the life of the product.

There’s no safe level of lead for a child. The CDC’s blood lead reference value has been lowered twice because every time they looked harder, they found effects at lower levels. Small, repeated oral exposure in a two-year-old is exactly the scenario the science is worried about.


## What the notice doesn’t say

Read the recall page and you’ll notice something missing: a number. The notice says the zipper contains lead “above the federal limit.” It doesn’t say how far above. Twice the limit? Fifty times? That’s the difference between a technical violation and a real hazard, and CPSC isn’t publishing it.

That’s a pattern with consumer product recalls, and it leaves parents in the worst position: told to worry, not told how much. If your kid has been using one of these for two years, the honest answer is that you can’t tell from the notice whether that matters, and the only way to find out is a blood lead test through the pediatrician. That’s a phone call, not a panic.

Check your imports. Cheap metal hardware on kids’ products from marketplace sellers is the recurring theme in lead recalls, and the marketplaces aren’t testing it before it ships.


## What we know

- SQT recalled ~1,835 Lontcs busy boards on September 10, 2026; sold on Amazon May 2024–July 2026, ~$31.
- Zipper contains lead above the federal lead paint ban limit. No injuries reported.
- Remedy: full refund; disassemble, mark “RECALLED,” email photo, dispose.


## What we don’t know yet

The lead concentration in the zipper. CPSC did not publish it.
