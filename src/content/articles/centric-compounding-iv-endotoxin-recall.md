On September 13, Centric Compounding issued a nationwide recall of three injectable products, glutathione, Myer’s Cocktail, and Tri-Immune Boost, for elevated endotoxin levels. These are the IV drips sold at wellness clinics and med spas: vitamins, antioxidants, and immune boosters delivered straight into a vein.

If you’ve had one recently and you’re wondering whether to worry: check the lot against the FDA notice, and if you had a fever, chills, or felt like you had the flu within hours of an infusion, that’s a call to a doctor. Endotoxin in the bloodstream causes exactly that reaction, and at high enough doses it causes septic shock.


## What endotoxin is

Endotoxin is a piece of the outer membrane of gram-negative bacteria. It’s not the bacteria themselves. When the bacteria die, the membrane fragments stay behind, and the fragments are what the immune system reacts to. Sterilization, by heat, filtration, or chemistry, kills the organism. It doesn’t remove the endotoxin. You can have a perfectly sterile product, no living bacteria in it at all, that will put someone in the hospital because of what the bacteria left behind before they were killed.

That’s why endotoxin is tested separately from sterility in injectable manufacturing, and why an elevated endotoxin result means something specific: at some point before sterilization, gram-negative bacteria were growing in the product or in something that touched it. The water system. A surface. The air handling in the compounding suite. A raw material stored wet.


## Why an environmental person cares about a pharmacy recall

Because it’s an environmental-controls failure that showed up as a product recall. Compounding pharmacies operate cleanrooms, with controlled air, monitored surfaces, and water systems that have to meet purity specs. Endotoxin in the finished product means one of those controls broke, and the monitoring didn’t catch it before the product shipped.

That’s the same discipline as any controlled environment: the manufacturing suite, the hospital pharmacy, the semiconductor fab. Air sampling, surface sampling, water sampling, on a schedule, with action limits. When it works, nobody hears about it. When it doesn’t, it’s a recall notice with three product names and a lot of clinics making phone calls.


## What we know

Centric Compounding issued a nationwide recall of glutathione, Myer’s Cocktail, and Tri-Immune Boost injectables on September 13, 2026 for elevated endotoxin levels.


## What we don’t know yet

- The source of the contamination in the compounding process.
- Whether any adverse events have been reported.
