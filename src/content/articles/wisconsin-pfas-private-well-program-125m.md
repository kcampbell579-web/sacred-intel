Wisconsin launched two PFAS testing programs on October 20, funded from the state’s $125 million PFAS Trust Fund.

The Private Well Testing Program mails eligible homeowners a sampling kit with a prepaid label addressed to the Wisconsin State Laboratory of Hygiene. The homeowner takes the sample, drops it in the mail, and gets a lab result. The Community Grants Sampling Program lets local governments pay for testing of private wells, some school and childcare water supplies, and wastewater. And the expanded Well Compensation Grant Program can cover a treatment system, a replacement well, or a connection to public water where contamination turns up. Agricultural and irrigation well funding is slated for 2027.

A state is now paying for mail-in private well sampling at scale. That’s the sentence.


## Why private wells are the gap


Private wells aren’t covered by the Safe Drinking Water Act. No utility, no required testing, no annual report. The homeowner is the water system. Most have never tested for PFAS because it’s expensive, because they don’t know they should, and because until recently there wasn’t anywhere convenient to send a sample. Delaware launched a free program in September. Wisconsin’s is bigger and it’s built around the mail.

The design choices are the part worth reading. Prepaid shipping, so cost isn’t a reason not to. An accredited state lab, so the number means something. A defined next step if the number’s high, so a bad result isn’t just bad news. Those three things are what separate a testing program from a pamphlet.


## New York


New York has roughly a million private wells, concentrated upstate and on the East End of Long Island. It has PFAS plumes at Hoosick Falls, Newburgh, Gabreski, Calverton, Bethpage, and now East Hampton. It has a state lab with PFAS capacity. It has settlement money coming in from the AFFF litigation. What it doesn’t have is a program that mails a kit to a well owner in Bridgehampton and says “send it back, we’ll pay.”

Suffolk County did door-to-door sampling in the East Hampton flow path this fall, 26 households, which is good and small. The Wisconsin model is “any eligible well, any resident.” I’d like to see New York steal it. The people who most need the test are the ones least likely to pay for it, and a state program is how they get it.

One more design detail: the program covers schools and childcare water supplies through the community grants. Those are the places where a private well serves a hundred kids instead of one family, and where nobody’s checked because the building isn’t a regulated public water system. That’s a category New York should be looking at regardless of what it does for households.


## What the state programs set as the standard


For anyone who sells or buys a mail-in kit, Wisconsin’s program is now the consumer expectation: prepaid shipping, an accredited lab, a named method, and a clear answer on what to do next. A kit that doesn’t deliver all four is competing with a free program that does. That’s fair. It’s also the right bar.

## What we know

- Wisconsin DNR launched a Private Well Testing Program (mailed kits, prepaid label to the Wisconsin State Laboratory of Hygiene) and a Community Grants Sampling Program, announced October 20, 2026, funded from the state’s $125 million PFAS Trust Fund.
- The expanded Well Compensation Grant Program may cover treatment systems, replacement wells, or public water connections where contamination is found.
- Agricultural and irrigation well funding expected in 2027.

## What we don’t know yet

- Eligibility criteria and program capacity.
- Which PFAS analytical method the state lab is using.
