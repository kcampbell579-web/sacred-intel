A federal judge is evaluating whether the proposed $7.25 billion glyphosate settlement is fair to the people with cancer claims. Individual awards under the deal would run from about $10,000 to $165,000, depending on the claim. Bayer would keep selling glyphosate-based weed killers. The fairness hearing is about whether that compensation structure adequately reflects the strength of the exposure-and-disease claims underneath it.

I’m not going to tell you whether glyphosate causes non-Hodgkin lymphoma. The science is contested in a way that PFAS and asbestos are not, the regulatory agencies disagree with each other, and the juries have gone both ways. What I’ll tell you is what a settlement that keeps the product on the market means for the people I work with.


## The exposure is ordinary, and undocumented

Glyphosate is the most-used herbicide in the world. It’s in the landscaper’s backpack sprayer, the grounds crew’s truck, the highway department’s right-of-way program, and the homeowner’s garage. Most of the people applying it are not licensed applicators. Almost none of them keep a record of what they sprayed, when, how much, or what they were wearing.

A settlement that preserves the product means the exposure continues. Which means the question “did this cause my cancer” will keep being asked, for decades, by people with no record of their exposure to answer it with. And it means the boring advice is still the right advice: if your crews apply it, log it. Product, date, quantity, PPE, re-entry interval. It costs nothing, and it’s the difference between a claim and a story.

For the worker: gloves, long sleeves, don’t eat until you’ve washed, and treat the re-entry interval on the label as real. Whether or not the lawsuit is right about the cancer, the label is right about the skin irritation and the eye damage, and those are today’s problem.


## What we know

- A federal judge is evaluating the fairness of a proposed $7.25 billion glyphosate settlement (The New Lede, September 14, 2026).
- Individual awards would range from ~$10,000 to $165,000. Bayer would continue selling glyphosate-based products.


## What we don’t know yet

Whether the judge approves the settlement as proposed.
