Pacific Health Sciences voluntarily recalled one lot of 4-ounce Babies’ Magic Tea Baby Sleep Gripe Water after it was found to contain undeclared alcohols: ethanol, methanol, and isopropanol. FDA posted the recall October 7, alongside a preworkout powder containing DMAA and a batch of broccoli seeds with possible Salmonella.

Gripe water is a traditional remedy for infant colic, sold as an herbal liquid, given by dropper to babies. Methanol is wood alcohol. In an adult it causes blindness and death at small doses; the toxic amount for an infant is proportionally tiny. Isopropanol is rubbing alcohol. Neither has any business near a baby, and neither was on the label.


## How alcohols get into a product that doesn’t list them


Three usual routes. A contaminated raw material, often a botanical extract made with industrial solvent that wasn’t fully removed. A manufacturing error, the wrong drum. Or fermentation in a product with sugar and no preservative, which produces ethanol but not methanol or isopropanol, so that’s unlikely here. The presence of all three points at a raw material or a process, and FDA’s lab found it, not a consumer complaint.


## Why this is the one to remember


Because the ingredient list on a consumer product is what the manufacturer says is in it. It’s not a test result. For a supplement, which gripe water is regulated as, nobody verifies the label before the product ships; FDA tests after, sometimes, when there’s a reason. The label is a claim.

That’s true of the lawn product’s “inert ingredients,” the “PFAS-free” rain jacket, and the “natural” sleep remedy. The only thing that turns the claim into a fact is an analysis, and for most products nobody runs one.

If you have this product: check the lot against the FDA notice. If an infant has had any of it and shows unusual drowsiness, vomiting, or trouble breathing, that’s an emergency room, and bring the bottle.

## What we know

- Pacific Health Sciences voluntarily recalled one lot of 4 oz Babies’ Magic Tea Baby Sleep Gripe Water for undeclared ethanol, methanol, and isopropanol (FDA, October 7, 2026).

## What we don’t know yet

- Lot number and distribution; see the FDA notice. Measured concentrations. Source of the contamination.
