CPSC dropped a batch of children’s product recalls on September 3. I’m walking through them because at least one of these is in a house you know, and because each one is a small lesson in how a hazard gets past everybody until it doesn’t.


## The list

Skip Hop Sesame Street Elmo silicone teethers. About 22,660 units. The black eyes can detach when the teether is frozen. That’s a choking hazard, and CPSC’s language is “serious injury or death,” which is CPSC for “we’re not kidding.” Here’s what gets me: freezing a teether is the entire point of a teether. Cold soothes the gums. So the failure happens under the exact normal use the product is sold for. This isn’t a kid doing something weird. It’s the product doing what it was designed for and coming apart.

GIHNJSI “Squeezy Dumplings” toys. They contain water beads. Water beads are tiny polymer spheres that swell dramatically when wet. A toddler swallows one dry, it hits stomach fluid, it expands, and now you have an intestinal obstruction that doesn’t show up on an X-ray because the bead is mostly water. Kids have died. Kids have had bowel resections. This is not a new hazard and it keeps showing up because the toys keep getting made and the marketplaces keep listing them.

SHEIN spiral toys. Violate the federal small-parts ban. Straightforward. A part small enough to fit in a test cylinder is small enough to block an airway.

Six-drawer dressers from Nanjing Wu Hai and Gizoon Direct. Fail STURDY Act stability requirements. This is the one I’d put on a podcast.


## The dresser problem, specifically

The STURDY Act set a mandatory tip-over standard for clothing storage furniture in 2023. Before that, the industry ran on a voluntary standard, and the voluntary standard was written with the assumption that drawers would be closed and nobody would be climbing. Which is a great assumption if you’ve never met a three-year-old. The mandatory standard tests with drawers open, with weight in them, on carpet, with a simulated climbing child. It’s a much better test.

It’s been the law for three years. And dressers keep failing it, because a dresser that passes costs more to make, and the ones that don’t are cheaper on a marketplace listing that nobody’s checking before it ships. Every one of these recalls means a dresser got manufactured, listed, sold, delivered, assembled, and put in a kid’s room before anyone found out. The recall is the system working. The three years before the recall are the system not working.


## What to actually do

Anchor every piece of furniture in a kid’s room to the wall. Dressers, bookshelves, TV stands, the changing table. Regardless of what the box says, regardless of whether it “passed.” Anti-tip straps are about twelve dollars for a pack and take ten minutes with a drill. It’s the single highest-return safety fix in a house with small children and most people don’t do it because the furniture “seems heavy.” Heavy is not the same as stable. A 100-pound dresser with three drawers open is a lever.

Check your freezer teethers. If it’s the Skip Hop Elmo, it’s out. If it’s any other silicone teether with attached decorative parts, look at it under a light after freezing. Anything that’s an add-on can be a come-off.

Water beads: just no. Not in the house. Not in the sensory bin at the daycare. Not in the flower vase, which is where a lot of them come from. If there’s a kid under five in the house, they don’t belong there. There’s no supervision level that makes a dry bead on the floor safe.

Small parts test. If you don’t have the cylinder, a toilet paper tube is close enough. If a piece fits through, it’s a choking risk for a kid under three.

Check the CPSC recall page monthly if you have kids under five. It takes three minutes. Set a reminder. It’s not paranoid, it’s the same thing you’d do for a car.


## What we know

- CPSC recalls announced September 3, 2026: Skip Hop Elmo teethers (~22,660), GIHNJSI Squeezy Dumplings (water beads), SHEIN spiral toys (small parts), and six-drawer dressers from Nanjing Wu Hai and Gizoon Direct (STURDY Act).
- STURDY Act mandatory stability requirements took effect in 2023.


## What we don’t know yet

- Unit counts for the water bead and dresser recalls; check CPSC’s individual notices.
- Whether any injuries have been reported for these specific products.
