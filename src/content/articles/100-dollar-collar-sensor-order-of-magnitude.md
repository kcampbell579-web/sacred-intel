Colorado State University researchers, working with the Forest Service, built a solar-powered particulate logger that bolts onto existing wildlife GPS collars for about $100 in parts. It uses the same low-cost laser sensor as a PurpleAir, logs PM2.5 every 30 minutes, and sits on the animal until the collar’s retrieved. Published in Methods in Ecology and Evolution.

In the first mule deer deployments, the readings on the animal differed from the closest official monitoring station by about an order of magnitude. Urban deer picked up minor traffic-related spikes the station never saw.


## Why a wildlife paper is an exposure paper


Because the deer is a stand-in for a person. The regulatory monitor is a fixed box on a rooftop or a fence, siting-approved, maintained, and representative of the regional air by design. It’s not representative of any particular spot, and it was never meant to be. A person, or a deer, moves through microenvironments: a road, a canyon that traps smoke, a draw where the inversion settles, a kitchen. The dose they get is the integral of all of those, and the station measures none of them.

Ten times off isn’t a sensor failure. It’s the gap between area monitoring and personal exposure, which industrial hygiene has known about for fifty years and which the public conversation about air quality mostly ignores. The AQI on your phone is the station. Your lungs are the collar.


## The use


When a client, or a project manager, or a school district points at the county AQI reading as evidence of what a worker or a kid actually breathed, this is the paper. A personal sampler on the person is the measurement. The station is context. They can differ by a factor of ten and both be right.

The $100 part matters too. A low-cost sensor isn’t a reference monitor and can’t be used for compliance. But a dozen of them on a crew, or around a school, or on a fenceline, for the price of one site visit, tell you where the exposure is uneven. Then you put the real sampler there.

## What we know

- Colorado State University / U.S. Forest Service open-source PM2.5 logger for wildlife collars, ~$100 in materials, low-cost laser sensor, 30-minute logging (Methods in Ecology and Evolution; reported October 8, 2026).
- First mule deer deployments differed from the nearest official monitoring station by ~an order of magnitude; urban deer registered minor traffic-related spikes.

## What we don’t know yet

- Sensor accuracy validation against a reference instrument in the field.
