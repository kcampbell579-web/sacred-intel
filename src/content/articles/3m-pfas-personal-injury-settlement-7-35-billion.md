3M signed a settlement on November 3 to resolve the personal-injury side of the firefighting-foam PFAS litigation: up to $7.35 billion, paid out through 2034, which 3M books at $5.8 billion in today’s dollars. It covers roughly 115,000 to 120,000 people who have one of six diagnoses and can show they were exposed to PFAS, either by drinking contaminated water or by handling the foam itself. 3M admits nothing. The company says it is “prepared to continue to defend itself” if enough people don’t sign.

The next morning, 3M stock rose as much as 10 percent, its highest level since March 2018. UBS told clients investors had been bracing for something closer to $10 billion. The company announced a $6 billion share buyback alongside the settlement.

I want to be careful here, because “company settles, stock goes up” is a story people tell with a lot of outrage and not much math. So here’s the math.


## What the deal actually is


This is not a class action and there’s no judge deciding whether it’s fair. It’s a private agreement. Every eligible person gets an election form and chooses one of three things: take the deal and sign a release, decline and stop litigating, or decline and keep fighting 3M on their own. Anyone who doesn’t respond gets a show-cause order and a dismissal with prejudice, which is legal for “your case is over and you can’t refile it.”

The six covered diseases are kidney cancer, testicular cancer, liver cancer, thyroid cancer, ulcerative colitis, and thyroid disease. That list isn’t random; it’s the set where the epidemiology is strongest, and it closely tracks what the C8 Science Panel found in West Virginia and Ohio after the DuPont litigation. The settlement agreement goes further and has plaintiffs’ counsel affirm that “current science does not support association” between PFAS and anything else. If you have pancreatic cancer and a well full of PFOA, this deal is not for you.

Exposure comes two ways. “Drinking water” means water for drinking, cooking, or bathing from a public system or a private well. “Direct exposure” means everything else, which in practice means firefighters who handled AFFF. Living near a base or an airport doesn’t qualify on its own; you need the water pathway.

Awards are point-scored on diagnosis plus exposure, and the scoring sheet isn’t public. One independent estimate puts the average around $56,000 to $58,000 per person before legal fees. For a kidney cancer, that’s not a lot of money. For a vetted pool that includes thyroid disease and ulcerative colitis, it’s a mixed-severity average, and the actual checks will be very different from each other.


## Where the money is, and when


Here’s the part the headline hides. 3M pays $50 million within 30 days of signing. It pays nothing in 2027. Six main payments of $1.045 billion each run from January 2028 to July 2033. The rest, about $570 million, is contingent: participation bonuses, threshold payments, and a reserve fund that 3M doesn’t owe at all if the litigation is terminated.

So the honest number is “up to $7.35 billion,” which is the phrase plaintiffs’ lawyers used. The $5.8 billion present value is 3M’s own accounting for the fact that a dollar paid in 2033 is worth less than a dollar paid now. And 3M’s lawyers said on the investor call that the payments should be tax-deductible, which the $5.8 billion doesn’t account for.

Two more things. 3M can terminate if participation doesn’t hit confidential thresholds; the company’s chief legal officer said they don’t expect that to happen. And the release each person signs covers any 3M product containing PFAS, not just foam. A firefighter who takes this deal appears to be releasing turnout-gear claims against 3M too. That’s my reading of the agreement; neither side has said it out loud, and the turnout-gear litigation is a separate MDL in Minnesota.


## What it doesn’t touch


This is 3M only. DuPont, Chemours, Corteva, Tyco, and BASF have all settled with water utilities and none of them has settled a single personal-injury claim. The state attorney-general suits are untouched, and 3M itself named those as the next big exposure. Water systems that opted out of the 2023 deal, airports, property owners, and the Australian and Canadian cases are all still open.

The first personal-injury bellwether trial, a kidney cancer case, was supposed to start in October 2025. It got vacated after a late surge of filings and was never rescheduled. Plaintiffs’ counsel is pretty direct that the “looming trials” are what brought 3M to the table. That’s probably right, and it’s also why 3M bought a stay of all PI proceedings against it the day after signing.


## Why this is a Long Island story


Suffolk County has Francis S. Gabreski Airport in Westhampton, where AFFF was used for decades and where the plume reached private wells. It has the former Grumman site in Bethpage. It has the Suffolk County Water Authority, which was one of the utilities in the 2023 settlement. The people who drank that water for thirty years and now have one of the six diagnoses are exactly who this agreement is written for, and the election forms are going to land in mailboxes here.

If that’s you or a parent: the deadline to be identified as an eligible claimant was reportedly November 13, which means your lawyer already had to have you on a list. The status hearing is November 20. Read the release before you sign it, and ask specifically what it does to any claim you have that isn’t about foam.


## The thing I actually want you to take from this


3M said in 2022 it would stop making PFAS by the end of 2025, and it says it did. The three PFAS deals it’s signed, water utilities, personal injury, and New Jersey, add up to a little over $20 billion in nominal commitments. That is a very large number and, judged by what the stock did, a smaller one than the market was braced for.

The water settlement paid for treatment. This one pays people who got sick. What neither one does is tell you whether the water at your house, today, has PFAS in it. The utilities tested their systems. Nobody tested the pipe between the main and your kitchen, and nobody tested a private well they weren’t asked to. That remains your job, and I say that as someone who has watched this litigation for a decade and still had to run my own.

## What we know

- 3M signed a Master Settlement Agreement November 3, 2026 in the AFFF MDL (No. 2:18-mn-2873, D.S.C., Judge Richard Gergel): up to $7.35 billion nominal, 2026–2034; $5.8 billion present value booked as a Q4 2026 charge.
- Six eligible injuries: kidney, testicular, liver, and thyroid cancer; ulcerative colitis; thyroid disease. Exposure via drinking water (public or private well) or direct handling of AFFF.
- About 115,000–120,000 eligible plaintiffs; 3M says ~99% of current PI claims.
- Payment schedule: $50M in 2026; $0 in 2027; six payments of $1.045B from January 2028 to July 2033; roughly $570M contingent (threshold, bonus, reserve).
- No admission of liability; 3M may terminate if confidential participation thresholds aren’t met (deadline December 15, 2029).
- Judge Gergel stayed PI proceedings against 3M November 4; status hearing November 20.
- 3M stock rose as much as 10% November 4 to its highest since March 2018; UBS said investors expected ~$10B; 3M announced a $6B buyback the same day.
- Separate: 2023 water-supplier settlement, $10.5–12.5B nominal ($10.3B present value), paid 2024–2036.

## What we don’t know yet

- The point-scoring sheet for awards, including whether turnout-gear exposure counts as direct exposure.
- The sealed participation thresholds.
- Whether the November 13 identification date and the 60/120-day deadlines for non-participants are exactly as reported by secondary trackers; the court orders themselves weren’t publicly posted when this was written.
