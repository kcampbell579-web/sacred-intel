Suffolk County Executive Ed Romaine announced that NextEra has suspended operations at the East Hampton Energy Storage Center and a companion facility in Montauk. The trigger: NYSDEC identified the East Hampton site’s fire-suppression system as the probable source of perfluoropropionic acid, PFPrA, in groundwater and in Suffolk County Water Authority wells at the Buckskill Road wellfield. Three of four wells there are out of service.

I’ve been writing about this plume since September. What’s new is the shutdown, the well count, and one sentence in the coverage that’s the most important thing in it: there is no drinking water standard written for PFPrA specifically. New York’s catch-all limit of 50 ppb for unspecified organic contaminants applies, and that’s what took the wells offline. But 50 is a placeholder, not a toxicology number, so the state Health Department set a site-specific screening value of 3 parts per billion.


## The contaminant with no standard


Every number you’ve ever seen on a water report is a comparison. The lead result against the 15 ppb action level. The nitrate result against 10 mg/L. PFOA and PFOS against 4 parts per trillion. The standard is what makes a result mean something. Without it, a concentration is a number on a page.

PFPrA has no standard. It’s an ultra-short-chain PFAS, three carbons, that formed when about 4,900 pounds of Novec 1230 fire suppressant met a million-plus gallons of water during the 2023 fire. It wasn’t on anyone’s list because nobody expected it in drinking water. There’s no federal MCL, no New York MCL, no health advisory. When SCWA finally got a method that could see it, the lab report came back with a concentration and nothing to compare it to.

So the Health Department made one. A site-specific screening value is what an agency produces when it has to say “this well is on or off” and the rulebook is blank. The toxicologists pull whatever animal studies exist for the compound or its closest relatives, apply uncertainty factors for the gaps, estimate a daily intake that’s probably safe, and back-calculate to a water concentration. For PFPrA the data is thin, the relatives are other short-chain PFAS with their own thin data, and the result is 3 ppb. That’s 3,000 parts per trillion, which sounds generous next to the 4 ppt for PFOA until you remember that short-chain compounds are generally less bioaccumulative and the uncertainty cuts both ways.

Is 3 ppb right? Nobody knows. It’s defensible, it’s documented, and it’ll be revised when there’s more data. That’s what a screening value is. The wells that came back at 10,000 ppb at the source and 1,000 near the wellfield are far enough over it that the exact number didn’t matter for the shutdown decision. It’ll matter for the private wells at the plume’s edge.


## Why the suspension matters


Two facilities offline is a utility and a county saying the risk from the fire-suppression design is real enough to stop the plants until it’s addressed. That’s not a finding of fault; it’s a precaution, and NextEra would say so. But grid-scale battery storage is being built across the island on the strength of the same suppression chemistry, and a county executive shutting two of them down over groundwater is a signal to every siting board with an application in front of it.


## For the East End


Three of four wells at Buckskill is a supply problem SCWA will manage by moving water around. For anyone on a private well in the area, the questions haven’t changed since September: test with a method that sees short-chain compounds, ask the lab for PFPrA by name, and compare the result to 3 ppb, knowing that number is a placeholder. And expect realtors to start asking, because a well test is about to become part of every East End closing.

## What we know

- NextEra suspended operations at the East Hampton Energy Storage Center and a Montauk facility after NYSDEC identified the East Hampton site’s fire-suppression system as the probable PFPrA source (Suffolk County Executive announcement October 5, 2026; Energy-Storage.news October 7).
- Three of four SCWA wells at the Buckskill Road wellfield out of service (East Hampton Town).
- No compound-specific drinking water standard exists for PFPrA; New York’s 50 ppb unspecified organic contaminant MCL applies. NYS DOH set a site-specific screening value of 3 ppb.
- PFPrA is a breakdown product of 3M’s Novec 1230, from the May 2023 fire.

## What we don’t know yet

- The basis document for the 3 ppb screening level.
- Conditions for restarting the facilities.
