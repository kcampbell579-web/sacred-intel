The Guardian reports growing community opposition to data center wastewater discharges across the U.S. The issue isn’t that data centers use water, which everyone knows. It’s what the water looks like when it leaves.

Data centers run hot, and cooling them at scale means cooling towers, chillers, and closed loops that have to be treated to keep them from growing biofilm and corroding. So the water picks up biocides (the chemicals that keep Legionella and algae down), corrosion inhibitors, scale inhibitors, and whatever metals leach from the system. Then it gets blown down, discharged, and it goes either to a municipal sewer or to a river. Local treatment plants in several regions say they’re being overwhelmed by both the volume and the chemistry.


## Why this is a new category

Industrial discharge is regulated, and most of the big categories have decades of history: refineries, paper mills, metal finishing. The permits know what to expect. Data center cooling water doesn’t have that history. It looks like cooling tower blowdown from a commercial building, but at a scale that a commercial building never reaches, and with a treatment chemistry that varies by operator. A municipal plant designed for residential sewage with a little light industry can be knocked sideways by a sudden, large, chemically complex stream.

There isn’t a standard consultant playbook for it yet. There’s no clear sampling protocol, no accepted list of what to test for, no regional pretreatment standard tuned to it. That’s a gap.


## Why I’m flagging it for Long Island

Because there’s an active data center development pipeline here, and the receiving environment is a sole-source aquifer and a set of bays that already struggle with nitrogen. Whatever gets permitted is going to discharge to a sewer district or to groundwater, and the sewer districts are going to have to figure out pretreatment limits for a chemistry they haven’t seen.

Early positioning is worth more than late expertise on this one. If you do discharge or pretreatment work, start reading what the operators are using. If you sit on a town board, ask the applicant what’s in the blowdown before you ask how many jobs.


## What we know

- The Guardian (September 8, 2026) reports escalating community opposition to data center wastewater discharges carrying biocides, metals, and treatment chemicals.
- Local treatment plants in several regions report being overwhelmed by volume and chemistry.


## What we don’t know yet
