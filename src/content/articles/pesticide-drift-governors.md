First, sourcing. Beyond Pesticides is an advocacy organization. That’s not a knock, it’s a label, and you should know it before you read the next paragraph. On September 8 they published a call for governors to prohibit drift-prone pesticides and any product applied outside its label restrictions. They cite a Cape Charles, Virginia resident who says she was sickened by a neighboring farm’s applications and got no response from state agriculture enforcement. I haven’t seen the complaint file, so I’m not going to tell you what happened in Virginia.

Here’s what I will tell you, because it isn’t advocacy. It’s how risk assessment works.

Label-based risk assumes the stuff goes where it’s aimed. When EPA sets the “acceptable” risk for a pesticide, the model assumes on-target application at the labeled rate, under labeled conditions, by a licensed applicator following the label. Drift, the fraction that blows off-target onto the house next door, is by definition a label violation. So it’s not in the math. It’s not that EPA thinks drift is safe. It’s that the model doesn’t have a box for it.

Which means the exposure at the property line is, formally, unmeasured. Not low. Not high. Unmeasured. Those are different words and the difference is the whole story.

Why a construction safety person cares about a farm story. Because it’s the same failure mode I see every week. A “contained” asbestos abatement. A dust control plan that assumes the water truck actually runs. A remediation with a perimeter that exists on the site plan and nowhere else. Every risk model has an assumption about containment, and every assumption is a place where the real number can be wildly different from the modeled one. The only way to close the gap is to measure it. Air sampling at the fence line. Surface wipes on the neighbor’s porch. Numbers, not assumptions.

So take the policy ask however you like. The technical point stands on its own: when a model says “acceptable,” ask what it assumed, and then ask whether anybody checked.


## What we know

- Beyond Pesticides (advocacy organization) published a call on September 8, 2026 for governors to prohibit drift-prone pesticides and off-label applications.
- The group cites a Cape Charles, VA resident’s complaint that reportedly received no state enforcement response.
- EPA pesticide risk assessments are based on labeled, on-target application.


## What we don’t know yet

- The facts of the Virginia case.
- Whether any governor intends to act.
