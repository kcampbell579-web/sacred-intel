A report from the Houston Advanced Research Center, covered by the Texas Tribune, looks at what at least 288 planned data center projects would do to Texas air. Even under a cleaner grid with more renewables, statewide nitrogen oxides could rise 35 percent by 2031. If facilities run on on-site natural gas, the picture is far worse: volatile organic compounds exceeding 2002 levels by 32 percent, and particulate matter back to where it was in 2006.

Two decades of air quality progress, undone by one industry’s buildout. The report ties the NOx to chronic lung disease and the particulate to asthma attacks, arrhythmia, and respiratory illness, which is the standard literature and not in dispute.


## The pathway that matters locally


Data centers don’t emit much when they’re running on grid power. They emit when the grid doesn’t: diesel backup generators, dozens per facility, tested monthly and run for real during outages. And they emit all the time if they’re built with on-site gas generation, which more of them are, because the grid can’t deliver the power fast enough.

That’s a permitting question, a stack question, and a community monitoring question, and all three are arriving on Long Island, where industrial parcels are in play for the same buildout. The air permit for a data center is written around the generators. Whether the generators are Tier 4 diesel with aftertreatment or something older, how many hours a year they’re allowed, and whether anyone monitors at the fence, those are the numbers that decide whether the neighborhood two blocks away notices.

I wrote in September about the PFAS in data center cooling and suppression. This is the other half: what comes out of the stack. Ask about both before the megawatts.

## What we know

- HARC report (via Texas Tribune, October 20, 2026): 288+ planned Texas data center projects could raise statewide NOx 35% by 2031 even under a cleaner-grid scenario; under an on-site natural gas scenario, VOCs 32% above 2002 levels and PM at 2006 levels.

## What we don’t know yet

- Generator specifications and permitted hours for proposed Long Island facilities.
